Abstract. Memory is a critical component in LLM-based agents, enabling them to store and retrieve past executions. This empirical study focuses on memory addition and deletion. LLM agents display an experience-following property: high similarity between a task input and a retrieved memory record often results in highly similar agent outputs. Two challenges follow: error propagation (inaccuracies in past experiences compound) and misaligned experience replay. Regulating experience quality in the memory bank matters; future task evaluations can serve as free quality labels. This is the ACL 2026 experience-following study. Append-only stores without an update/override mechanism leave old and new facts coexisting.
